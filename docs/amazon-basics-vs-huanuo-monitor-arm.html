Amazon Basics vs HUANUO Monitor Arm Under $45 (2026 Pick)

Published November 07, 2026 · Monitors

Most budget gaming monitors ship with a stand that tilts and does nothing else. It sits too low, it eats the front third of your desk, and it wobbles every time you slam a key. A gas spring monitor arm fixes all three for less than the price of a new game, and it frees up the space your mouse actually needs for low-sensitivity aiming.

Two arms come up again and again at this price: the Amazon Basics Gas Spring Single Monitor Arm and the HUANUO Single Monitor Mount. They look almost the same in photos. They aren’t the same, and the difference that matters most isn’t one either brand advertises. It’s whether your monitor’s weight sits inside the arm’s working range.

This guide compares the two on that question, plus reach, warranty and price. We haven’t put either arm through a long-term test ourselves. The specs and prices below come from the live listings and price trackers we checked on the day of writing, and the downsides come from the manufacturers’ own notes and from published reviews.

The 30-second answer

  • Monitor 24 to 27 inches, weighing roughly 5 to 15 lbs without its stand: either arm works. Buy the Amazon Basics if it’s the cheaper of the two that day.
  • Monitor up to 32 inches, or heavier than 15 lbs: buy the HUANUO. The Amazon Basics isn’t rated for it.
  • You want the arm to last through two or three monitor upgrades: buy the HUANUO for its 5-year warranty.
  • Ultrawide or 34-inch curved screen: neither. See the used-market section below.

Meet the two arms

Amazon Basics Gas Spring Single Monitor Arm (model DS-MADSBE)

Around $36 at the time of writing

The Amazon Basics Gas Spring Single Monitor Arm is a single gas spring arm for 15–27 inch monitors weighing 4.4–15.4 lbs. It uses standard VESA 75x75 and 100x100 mounting. The listing gives up to 20.5 inches of extension, tilt from -90° to +85°, ±90° swivel, and full 360° rotation, so you can turn a monitor to portrait for a chat or Discord column. It mounts with a C-clamp or through a grommet hole on desks 0.39 to 4 inches thick. The arm weighs 4.18 lbs, comes in black, and carries a 1-year limited warranty. A price tracker had it at $35.99 on Amazon for the two weeks before we wrote this, with no movement.

Honest downsides: - The 15.4 lb ceiling is low. Most 24-inch gaming monitors fall under it once the stand comes off. Some 27-inch models, and anything bigger, may not. A monitor over the limit will slowly sink no matter how hard you tighten the spring. - It’s stiff out of the box. Amazon’s own listing warns that the joints are tightened for shipping and that you have to set the tension to your monitor’s weight. Skip that step and the arm will drift up or sag. - The warranty is only one year. That’s the shortest on this page, and gas springs are the part that wears out.

HUANUO Single Monitor Mount (model HNSS6)

Around $43 at the time of writing

The HUANUO Single Monitor Mount covers a wider range: 13–32 inch monitors weighing 4.4–19.8 lbs, again on VESA 75x75 and 100x100. It includes both a C-clamp and a grommet base. The Amazon listing we checked showed the white version (part number HNSS6W) at $42.99, down from a $49.99 list price, with a 5-year warranty. Published reviews of the HNSS6 also praise its tool-free VESA plate. You screw that plate to the monitor, then click the monitor onto the arm, which makes installing it alone much less awkward.

Honest downsides: - Light monitors can over-lift. Reviews summarized by DormGearHQ note that with a light monitor, the spring can push the arm to the top of its travel. The weight floor is a real limit, not a formality. - It’s shorter than premium arms. The same expert summaries say its reach is shorter than Ergotron’s. On a deep desk, you may not be able to push the monitor as far back as you’d like. - The finish looks budget. Reviewers describe the build as functional rather than premium. The joints and paint look like a $40 arm, because it is one.

The numbers most roundups skip

Price per pound of capacity. The HUANUO costs about $7 more, but it holds 4.4 lbs more. Per pound of rated capacity, that makes it slightly cheaper: about $2.17 against $2.34. If your monitor sits anywhere near 15 lbs, that extra headroom matters. A gas spring running at the very top of its range has no margin left as it ages.

Warranty cost per year. This is the bigger gap. Spread the price over the warranty and the Amazon Basics costs about $36 per covered year. The HUANUO costs about $8.60. Monitor arms usually outlive the monitor on them, so coverage that survives your next screen upgrade is worth more than the small price difference suggests.

Width of the weight window. A gas spring only works well inside its rated range. The HUANUO’s window is 15.4 lbs wide and the Amazon Basics’ is 11 lbs. A wider window means a better chance that your next monitor fits too.

The pitfall that ruins cheap arms: getting the weight wrong

Nearly every “my monitor arm sags” or “my monitor arm won’t stay down” complaint comes back to weight. Here’s how to get it right before you order:

  1. Weigh the panel, not the box. The weight printed on a monitor’s box or spec sheet often includes the stand, and the stand comes off. Check the spec sheet for a “without stand” figure. If there isn’t one, unscrew the stand and use a bathroom scale. Weigh yourself holding the monitor, then weigh yourself without it, and subtract.
  2. Aim for the middle of the range. If your panel sits near either end of an arm’s range, choose the arm where it lands closer to the middle. Near the top, the arm sags as the spring ages. Near the bottom, it over-lifts, which is the complaint reviewers raise about the HUANUO with light monitors.
  3. Check the VESA holes. Both arms need 75x75 or 100x100 mm screw holes on the back of the monitor. Most budget gaming monitors have them, but some slim or curved models don’t, or hide them behind the stand. Our guide to choosing a budget gaming monitor covers what to check on the spec sheet.
  4. Check the desk edge. A C-clamp needs a solid edge to grip. Desks with a thick rear lip, a hollow core, or a glass top can crack or flex under the clamp. Both arms also offer a grommet mount, which goes through a hole in the desk.

Which one for which setup?

  • A 24-inch, 144Hz starter monitor: Amazon Basics. Most of the screens in our roundup of 144Hz monitors under $150 come with tilt-only stands, and an arm is the cheapest way to get height adjustment. A 24-inch panel usually lands comfortably inside the Amazon Basics range.
  • A 27-inch now, maybe a 32-inch later: HUANUO. The size rating and the 5-year warranty both cover the upgrade.
  • A streaming desk with a webcam, light and mic: HUANUO. Raising the monitor clears space underneath for a mic arm, a key light clamp or a capture card. Our low-cost streaming starter guide covers the rest of that kit.

Want a premium arm? Look at the used market

Premium gas spring arms are one of the best things to buy used. They’re built to outlast the office desks they came from, and companies sell them off in bulk when they refurnish. Two searches worth bookmarking:

  • Ergotron LX — the well-known premium office arm that budget arms get compared to. Check that the listing includes the clamp, the screws and the VESA plate, and that the seller confirms the arm holds its position. Look up the rated weight range for the exact model number in the listing, because there are several versions.
  • Mount-It MI-1771 — another single gas spring arm in the same class as the two above. It’s worth a look if a used listing undercuts both new prices. Check the stated weight range against your monitor before you bid.

The used-market rule for arms is simple: a missing clamp or VESA plate can cost as much as the arm saved you. Only buy complete listings.

FAQ

Will a monitor arm make my monitor wobble more than the stock stand? It can, a little. A long arm clamped to a light desk passes vibration from typing more than a heavy stand on a wide base does. Tightening the joint tension and clamping close to a desk leg both help. In practice, most players accept a slight wobble in return for the right height and a clear desk.

Can I put a 34-inch ultrawide on either of these? No. The Amazon Basics is rated to 27 inches and the HUANUO to 32 inches. Ultrawides are also usually heavier and wider, which puts more leverage on the arm. Look at heavier-duty arms rated for ultrawides instead.

Do I need a grommet hole in my desk? No. Both arms include a C-clamp that grips the desk edge. The grommet base is an alternative for desks with a cable hole, or for desk edges that are too thick or too weak for a clamp.

Does a monitor arm help with aim? Not directly, but it helps your setup. With the monitor stand gone, you get more room to move the mouse, which matters if you play at low sensitivity. Pair the freed space with a larger pad. Our mouse pad size and surface guide explains how big to go.

Why does my new arm keep drifting up (or down)? The spring tension doesn’t match your monitor’s weight. Both arms let you adjust it. Turn the tension adjuster in small steps until the monitor stays where you leave it. If you can’t find a setting that holds, your monitor is probably outside the arm’s rated range.

Bottom line

If you have a typical 24-inch budget gaming monitor and want the cheapest reliable way to raise it and free up your desk, the Amazon Basics arm at around $36 does the job. Just respect its 15.4 lb ceiling and set the tension properly.

For about $7 more, the HUANUO at around $43 handles bigger and heavier screens, costs less per pound of capacity, and comes with a warranty five times as long. For most people building a desk they’ll keep upgrading, that makes it the better buy. Whichever you choose, weigh your monitor without its stand before you order. That one step prevents most monitor arm returns.
